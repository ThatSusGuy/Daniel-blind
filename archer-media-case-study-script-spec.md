# Case study video scripts: the Archer Media spec

An operational spec for writing case-study ad scripts in Daniel Iles's format, rebuilt on The Archer Media's own research: 78 prospects and 138 sales calls, Nov 2025 – Jul 2026.

**How to use this doc.** Fill the intake sheet (section 2), write line by line from the template (section 3), pull wording from the banks (section 4), apply the voice rules (section 5), and run the QA checklist (section 7) before anything ships. Section 8 is a finished example.

**Notation.** [SLOT] = fill in · {a / b} = choose one · (words) = optional · swap [he]/[his]/[him] for the client's pronouns, or for they/their/them when the script features a company.

---

## 1. The skeleton

**Length.** Target 350–450 words (about 2–2.5 minutes spoken) in 18–26 sentences.

**Proportions.** The transition lands at about 31% of the script, the first pillar at 36%, the scale proof at 87%, and the CTA at 92%. A 400-word script is roughly 145 words of story and proof, 205 words of method, and 50 words of close.

| Beat | Job for your viewer | Status |
|---|---|---|
| B1 Hook | One-sentence result in clients, revenue, or pipeline | Required |
| B2 Identity | Who they are, plus credibility or the "expert at X, not yet at getting clients" flip | Required, unless the hook already says what they do and there's nothing to add (see L2) |
| B3 Before | One or two problems, with a number | Required |
| B4 After | New numbers, plus an escalator only when there's a real overflow fact | Required |
| B5 Transition | "So here's the exact strategy..." | Required |
| B6 Pillars | 2–3 numbered moves | Required |
| B7 Risk line | Why the viewer isn't taking a blind bet: the new-client guarantee, if they qualify | Required |
| B8 Final state | Who they are now | Required |
| B9 Scale proof | "Hundreds of crores in sales pipeline..." | Optional (Daniel: 9 of 11) |
| B10 CTA | "Click the link down below, see if you qualify" | Required |

**Variant.** Results-last (B1, B2, B3, B6, B4, B8, B9, B10) is allowed. Use it when the headline is a revenue or ROI figure that lands best as the payoff of the method, or when the script must stay under about 300 words.

**Optional devices and their status:**

- **Meta-objection aside:** mandatory. 18 prospects believe their buyers aren't on Meta, and for 7 it's their core belief.
- **Escalator** ("so many X that Y"): only when the client's results overflowed. Daniel uses it in 3 of 11 scripts (Dr. Richard, Jared, Ashley), and the rule is in L7.
- **Pain math:** recommended. 20 prospects decide on ROI arithmetic, including 4 of the 7 buyers.
- **Anti-vanity "leads don't equal revenue":** recommended. Junk leads are a top-5 daily frustration (12).
- **Stakeholder win:** recommended. A hidden decision committee shows up in 25 prospects, and 9 deals stalled on an absent decision-maker.
- **Ownership close:** optional. Owning the growth engine is a deepest desire for 9, including 2 buyers.
- **Niche objection** ("too specialized for outsiders"): optional.
- **Also optional:** "sounds simple, but," superlative, iteration proof, first-big-deal breakout, stats board.

## 2. Intake sheet

Every number in the script comes from here. The template never invents a stat.

| Field | Collect | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| Name | Your choice, based on what the client has approved: the founder's first name, the company name, or anonymous. Daniel features a person, so use the founder when you can; use the company when an NDA rules the person out | Anonymous mode: "a [company type]" or "a [company type] founder" throughout |
| Company type | Plain English: "a custom software company," "an AI testing platform," "an IT services firm" | Required |
| Credibility | Marquee client names, years, team size, existing revenue | "already successful in the [industry] space" |
| Before behavior | How clients came in: referrals, founder selling, cold outreach, an agency, ads that produced junk | Required; sets the failure mode |
| Before numbers | Clients added per year, % of business from referrals, outreach volume and replies, months with a past agency, revenue plateau, share of junk leads | Use a zero ("had never run ads") or a minimizer ("one or two new clients a year") |
| After numbers | Qualified calls booked per month, new clients per month, revenue added, pipeline in lakhs/crores, average deal size, close rate, cost per booked call | Need at least two |
| Timeframe | Days, weeks, or months since starting | Required |
| Breakout moment | First deal closed from ads, and its size; or the biggest deal in company history | Skip L6 |
| Overflow fact | Did the demand outgrow the business? Had to hire a closer, couldn't take all the calls, cut other paid spend, started a waitlist | Skip the escalator |
| Channel doubt | Did they believe their buyers weren't on Meta? Their exact words if possible | Use the generic Meta aside |
| Skeptic | Who had to be convinced (partner, co-founder, board) and how they feel now | Skip the stakeholder win |
| Interventions | What the team actually did, mapped to pillar codes in 4C. The number of interventions sets the number of pillars | Required, 2–3 |
| Why it worked | For the main intervention: the reason it worked, in the client's words or yours ("buyers judge you by your offer before they ever get on a call") | Required for the hero pillar |
| Qualification | The criteria prospects had to meet before booking, what they were educated on, and any proof (share of calls qualified, show rate, deals closed on the first call) | Use the generic criteria line in 4C |
| Founder time now | Does the founder still take sales calls, and how many? | Skip the founder add-on in L13 |
| Superlative | Marquee logo landed, biggest deal in company history, category leader | Skip |
| CTA audience | B2B founders, software companies, IT services firms, SaaS companies | "B2B companies" |

**Missing-data rule.** If any of the following is missing, don't write the script. List what's missing and stop. Never fill a gap with a placeholder ("[AMOUNT]") or a vague stand-in ("crores in new revenue," "tons of new clients"). Daniel's scripts land because every claim is a specific number; a vague one reads as hype to prospects who decide on proof.

- who the script features: the founder's first name, the company name, or anonymous
- the company type
- the before behavior, with at least one number
- at least two after numbers, including the exact revenue figure if the script mentions revenue
- the timeframe
- the interventions (2–3 pillar codes)
- why the main intervention worked (the hero pillar needs it)
- what changed for the sales team, if the SALES pillar is used (it must end the method)
- the Meta proof number: how many of the new clients came from the ads

## 3. The line-by-line template

### L1 · Hook

Required. One sentence, 21–41 words (median 30).

> Here's how we took our client, [NAME], from [BEFORE] to [AFTER 1], [AFTER 2], and [AFTER 3] in just [TIMEFRAME].

- Pick the variant with the rule in 4A.
- [NAME] is whoever you've chosen to feature: the founder ("our client, Rohan") or the company ("our client, Luxhammer"). The founder is closest to Daniel, since all 11 of his scripts feature a person, but use the company whenever an NDA rules the person out. Pick one and stay with it for the whole script.
- Every result is an exact number: "11.6 crore," never "crores."
- Keep the frame exactly: "Here's how we," then "took" with from/to, or "helped" with a verb plus stats, then "our client, [NAME]."
- The default metric pair is new clients plus revenue (or pipeline). The third metric is qualified calls per month, deal size, or return on ad spend.
- Never put "leads" in the hook. Your prospects read it as junk.
- Use the client's own currency: lakhs and crores, or dollars if they bill in dollars.
- End on a time qualifier. "In just 90 days" is the default, and it answers the three-month-retainer burn ("They waste our money for three months, and then we had no results").
- Kickers: "without a single referral," "with zero cold calls," "on their very first campaign," "while [NAME] stepped out of sales completely," "and landed [MARQUEE CLIENT]."

### L2 · Identity and credibility

1–2 sentences.

**When to skip it.** Daniel skips a plain identity line when the hook already says what the client does, and keeps it only when it adds something: credibility, or the "expert at X, not yet at Y" flip. This holds in all 11 scripts. Jared ("plumber"), Stephanie ("e-com brand"), and Daryoosh ("attorney") all have it in the hook and go straight to the problem. Adam and Ashley also have it in the hook, but keep the line because it adds credibility or the flip. The other six hooks don't say what the client does, so all six have the line.

> So [NAME] runs a [COMPANY TYPE]. (Featuring a company: "So [NAME] is a [COMPANY TYPE]." Featuring a solo professional: "So [NAME] is a [PROFESSION].")
>
> So when [NAME] started working with us, [he] was already very successful in the [INDUSTRY] space, and [he] just wanted to scale the business without relying on referrals.
>
> When [NAME] came to us, [he] was an expert at [building software], but [he] wasn't yet an expert at getting clients.

- Start with "So" or "When," and name the company type by this line at the latest.
- Add a marquee client or credibility marker whenever you have one ("they work with Stryker"). 33 prospects anchor on named brands.
- Protect the client. Your prospects believe their product and closing are solved (26, including 5 of the 7 buyers). The problem is always the channel or the process, never the product, and never the people.
- If you use the third template ("expert at X, but wasn't yet an expert at getting clients"), the flip has already happened, so L3 must not open with "But." Daniel does exactly this: "When Adam came to us, he was an expert defense attorney, but he wasn't yet an expert on social media. His social media had no strategy."

### Setup rules (L2–L8)

These rules are measured across all 11 of Daniel's scripts, and they keep the opening paced like his.

- **One or two problems, never more.** Five of Daniel's setups name one problem and six name two. None names three. Two problems can share a sentence, joined with "and": Dr. Richard, Kevin, and Chelsea all do this ("he didn't post on social media consistently, and he didn't have a clear direction of what actually worked").
- **Every problem in the setup gets paid off,** either fixed by a pillar or shown solved in the after-state. Jared's 40 hours a month become four; Kevin's inconsistency is fixed by batch production. This holds in all 11.
- **A third problem goes inside the pillar that fixes it,** as that pillar's before-contrast, not in the setup. Jared's random posting appears in Pillar 1, Ashley's doing-everything-herself in Pillar 1, and Stephanie's missing conversion system in Pillar 3.
- **The after-state can name a result the setup never set up.** Jared's replaced ad spend appears only after the turnaround. So a problem cut from the setup can still show up solved ("and they've stopped depending on referrals").
- **Every "But" flips direction.** A sentence that opens with "But" must reverse the one before it: good to bad, or bad to good. All 14 sentence-opening "But"s across Daniel's scripts do this, as in Ashley's: "very successful coaching business. But her social media had no strategy... But now she's gone from..." Two "But" sentences in a row that point the same way is always wrong.

### L3 · Before: behavior

Required. Open with one of these, then add one failure-mode sentence from 4B. Put "But" in front only if L2 ended on a positive:

- "Before working with us,"
- "When [NAME] started working with us,"
- "When [NAME] came to us,"
- "Before coming to us,"

### L4 · Before: number

Required.

> [He] {was adding just [N] new clients a year / was getting 100% of [his] business from referrals / was reaching out to [N] people a month with almost no replies / had spent [N] months with an agency with zero results / had been stuck at [REVENUE] for [N] years}.

Shrink the number with "just," "only," "under," "a couple," or "never cracked." These come straight from your calls: "we are adding one client on an average per year," "All references, like 100% has been referenced so far," "stuck at the 2 million-ish mark."

### L5 · Before amplifier

Recommended. Use at most one.

- **Pain math:** "And at that pace, it would take over [N] years to hit [TARGET]." Compute (target − current) ÷ current yearly gain, round down, and prefix "over." Replaying a prospect's own numbers is one of your reps' best-performing moves (13).
- **Mirror:** "And this is something we see with B2B founders all the time. They're incredibly good at what they build, but they just don't have a system to get clients outside of referrals."
- **Founder constraint:** "But obviously, [he]'s the founder. [He]'s the one building the product. [He] doesn't have tons of time to be on sales calls all day. So we knew we had to come in and get [him] these results without [him] carrying the whole sales process."

### L6 · Breakout moment

Optional.

> But after just [N] weeks working with our team, [he] closed a [DEAL SIZE] deal and [his] pipeline just took off from there.

### L7 · After: numbers and escalator

The numbers are required. The escalator is conditional.

> But now [he] has over [N] qualified calls booked every month and [REVENUE] in new business.

Open with "But now," "Now," "But after working with us," or "Since working with us." Include 2+ numbers. You may repeat a hook number here (Daniel does in 7 of 11 scripts), but it isn't required. Revenue is always an exact figure.

**Repetition cap.** Each hook number appears at most twice: once in the hook, and once more, in the after-state or a pillar. Across Daniel's 11 scripts, only two numbers appear a third time: Dr. Richard's 100,000 followers, reused as the milestone in his pain math, and Jay's one-year goal, restated in a Goal Crush hook. Those are the only exceptions. Plan where each number lands: if the A8 Meta proof uses the client total ("14 of his 17 new clients"), don't also repeat that total in L7.

**Escalator, only with a real overflow fact:**

> ...and [he] started getting so many [calls / clients] that [he] actually [OVERFLOW FACT].

The rule, from all 11 of Daniel's scripts: the escalator appears when the client's demand overflowed, and that overflow isn't already the hook's headline. Dr. Richard's DMs were flooded, Jared replaced $60,000 a month in ad spend, and Ashley had to hire a sales team and change her business model; none of those is in their hooks. David also replaced his ad spend, but it's his hook's headline ("with zero ad spend"), so he doesn't repeat it as an escalator. The other seven report no overflow, and Daniel never invents one. Overflow facts for you look like:

- had to hire a closer to take all those calls
- couldn't take every call that came in
- cut [AMOUNT] a month in other paid spend
- started a waitlist for new clients

**Stats-board variant:** "So these right here are actually [his] first 30-day numbers. We booked [N] calls with decision-makers, [N] proposals went out, and [he] closed [N] new clients, again, just all in the first month. And now we've actually generated closer to [TOTAL] in pipeline, and probably a lot more by the time that you're watching this."

### L8 · After: ongoing rate

Use it when the intake has an ongoing rate (per week, per month, or per deal). Never invent one.

> And now [he] consistently {gets [N] qualified calls with decision-makers every single week / closes [N] new clients every single month}.

Daniel states an ongoing rate in 10 of his 11 scripts, but only when he has one; the eleventh (Daryoosh) has no ongoing figure, and Daniel doesn't make one up. If L7 already gives a monthly or per-deal rate (as in Greg's "an additional 10K a month"), that does the job, so skip L8 rather than repeating it. And if the closer you've picked already states the same rate, use it in only one place.

**Optional anti-vanity follow-up:** "But of course [he] didn't just want more leads. [He] wanted calls with decision-makers who were actually ready to buy."

### L9 · Transition

Required. Use one word for word, and don't reuse the same one in back-to-back scripts:

- "So here's the exact strategy that we used for [NAME]."
- "So here's exactly what we did to get [him] there."
- "Here's exactly how we helped [NAME] get there."
- "So here's how we helped [him] do that."
- "So here's the full strategy and breakdown of what we did to get [NAME] to this point."
- "So here's the exact strategy that blew [him] up in the first place."
- "And the exact strategy we used to do this is very simple to explain, but kind of difficult to execute if you have never had the experience of generating crores in pipeline before."

### L10–L12 · Pillars

Choose from the bank in 4C (OFFER, SWITCH, TARGET, QUALIFY, DFY, SALES, SYSTEM). Pillars are built from these parts:

- **(a) Label sentence with an ordinal.** P1: "First," / "So first," / "So the first thing we did was." P2: "Second," / "Next," / "So the second part of this is" / "We also." P3: "And third," / "And number three," / "And finally," / "And lastly."
- **(b) Before-contrast:** "Before, [he] was [OLD BEHAVIOR]."
- **(c) Specifics,** with a list of 3–6 concrete nouns: "the offer, the targeting, the ad creatives, the qualification, the follow-up."
- **(d) Optional aside.** The Meta aside must appear in P1 or P2.
- **(e) Outcome:** "that made them look like the obvious choice instead of just another vendor," or "They absolutely crushed." Optional in P1 and P2: 18 of Daniel's 30 pillars end on a result, with no consistent pattern in which ones do.
- **(f) Why it works.** Required in the hero pillar: one line on why the move works, a reason or principle, taken from the "Why it worked" intake field. Every one of Daniel's 11 hero pillars has one: "It gives people the wrong impression when they see your brand and they see poor quality content" (Jared), "He is a business owner, so it had to make sense for his schedule" (Adam), "His product basically sells itself" (David). This line is most of what makes Daniel's scripts feel full rather than thin.

**The last pillar must end on what changed.** This one holds in all 11 of Daniel's scripts: his method always closes on the client's new state, either in the last pillar's final line or in the sentence right after it. In your standard format the guarantee line comes right after the last pillar, so the last pillar's own final line has to carry it. In the results-last variant, the after-state comes right after the last pillar and carries the change instead, as in Greg's and Kevin's scripts.

**The label must match the body.** If the label says "a custom sales process," the body describes the sales process, not a new offer.

One of the first two pillars is the hero: 60–130 words, carrying the before-contrast and the aside. The other pillars run 25–80 words. P1 is always the failure mode's antidote (4B). QUALIFY is P2 by default. SALES is always last: it's the bonus that made the numbers bigger, not the fix, because most of your prospects believe their closing already works.

### L13 · Risk line

Required. This replaces Daniel's time-cost line, because sunk money with no bankable ROI is your prospects' #1 volunteered pain (15 fear it, including 2 buyers).

> And the best part is, you don't have to take a blind bet on us. If you qualify, we guarantee a 10 to 30 crore increase in revenue in 12 months, or your money back.

- This is the guarantee you give new clients, so it's the same line in every script, word for word. It's never about the featured client's own deal.
- Always include "if you qualify." The guarantee is never stated without it.
- Once per script, only here. Keep it to the one-line promise; no terms or conditions.
- Place it inside the DFY, SALES, or SYSTEM pillar, or right after the last pillar.
- **Founder add-on** (optional): "And now all [NAME] does is show up to the calls that are already booked. We handle everything else."

### L14 · Final state

Required. Pick one closer from 4E.

### L15 · Scale proof

Optional. Daniel uses one in 9 of 11 scripts, with no clear rule for the two that skip it. Your prospects decide on proof, so it's usually worth keeping.

> And we've generated hundreds of crores in sales pipeline for B2B companies just like [NAME]'s, and we do this every single day. (We have dozens of case studies and testimonials you can scroll through.)

Keep the parenthetical only if it's true. Verifiable proof is your prospects' #1 way of deciding (30).

### L16 · CTA

Required.

> And if [CONDITION from 4F], (just like we did for [NAME],) just click the link down below, see if you qualify.

"Click the link down below" never changes, and "see if you qualify" is the default. The condition must never mirror new-market or new-country expansion.

## 4. The banks

### 4A. Hook variants

The first matching rule wins.

1. **Goal Crush.** Use when the client had a stated target and beat it early. Client-count targets (16) and revenue milestones (10) are how your prospects dream.
   "Here's how we helped our client, [NAME], crush [his] [one year] goal of [GOAL] in just [ACTUAL TIME] and scaled to [BIGGER RESULT] in just [TIMEFRAME]."
2. **ROI.** Use when the headline is return on ad spend. 20 prospects decide on ROI math, including 4 buyers.
   "Here's how we helped [NAME] go from [relying on referrals] to generating a [X]x return from just [AMOUNT] of ad spend on [his] very first campaign."
3. **Descriptor.** Use when the company or its clients are more impressive than the name.
   "Here's how we helped a [software company that builds for a Fortune 500 brand] generate [REVENUE] and close [N] new clients in less than [TIMEFRAME]." Reveal the name in L2.
4. **From-To.** Use when there's a clear "from": "one client a year," "100% referrals," "stuck at 8 crore for three years," "1,500 cold emails a month."
   "Here's how we took our client, [NAME], from [BEFORE] to [AFTER 1], [AFTER 2], and [AFTER 3] in just [TIMEFRAME]."
    - Kickers: "without a single referral," "with zero cold calls," "while [NAME] stepped out of sales completely," "and landed [MARQUEE CLIENT]."
    - **Before-Contrast** is the two-sentence alternate when "for years" is the most striking fact: "Before coming to us, our client [NAME] had been [relying on referrals] for years and still hadn't [broken past REVENUE]. And less than [TIMEFRAME] after working with us, we had generated over [RESULT] for [his] [COMPANY TYPE] and made [him] one of the most recognizable names in [his] entire niche."
5. **Stat Stack.** Use for everything else.
   "Here's how we helped our client, [NAME], generate [REVENUE] in new revenue, {close [N] new clients / book [N] qualified calls a month}, and {build [PIPELINE] in pipeline / increase deal size by over [N]%} in just [TIMEFRAME]."

**Batch rotation.** Rotate hooks, transitions, closers, and CTAs across a series so it doesn't sound templated.

### 4B. Failure modes and the Pillar 1 each one requires

- **FM1 Referral ceiling.** 25 describe their problem this way at dinner, including 3 buyers; primary pain for 10.
  "[His] whole business was running on referrals and [his] network, and it was starting to dry up." **P1: OFFER.**
- **FM2 Founder trapped in sales.** 18 at dinner, including 3 buyers; primary pain for 4, including 2 buyers.
  "[He] was the only one doing sales. Every call, every proposal, every follow-up was [him]." **P1: DFY.**
- **FM3 Too few at-bats.** Starved top of funnel is the primary pain for 6 (1 buyer); 16 name lead volume as a daily frustration.
  "[His] team could close. They just didn't have enough calls to close." **P1: TARGET.**
- **FM4 Cold-outbound grind.** 11 tried outbound at volume; primary pain for 5.
  "[His] team was sending [N] cold emails a month and getting almost no replies." **P1: SWITCH.**
- **FM5 Junk leads.** A daily frustration for 12, including 2 buyers; primary pain for 4, including 1 buyer.
  "[He] was getting leads, but [N]% of them were junk." **P1: QUALIFY.**
- **FM6 Agency burn.** 15 at dinner, including 3 buyers.
  "[He] had already tried [N] agencies, and every single one took the retainer and delivered nothing." **P1: TARGET.** Don't comment on other agencies; Daniel never does. Let the guarantee in L13 answer the burn.
- **FM7 New offering, no go-to-market.** Primary pain for 6, including 1 buyer.
  "[He] had just built [PRODUCT], and there was no real plan for selling it." **P1: OFFER.**
- **FM8 Plateau or price-shopped.** Plateau is the primary pain for 3; price-shopped for 4.
  "[His] revenue had been stuck at [REVENUE] for [N] years, and buyers kept comparing [him] on price." **P1: OFFER.**
- **FM9 Concentration risk or no access to decision-makers.** Concentration is the primary pain for 4 (1 buyer); no access for 2 (1 buyer).
  "[N]% of [his] business came from one [client / partner], and [he] couldn't get in front of anyone else who actually signs." **P1: TARGET.**
- **FM10 Long sales cycles.** A daily frustration for 11.
  "Deals were taking [N] months to close, and half of them stalled out." **P1: QUALIFY.**

### 4C. Pillar bank

**Ordering constraints:**

- DFY is always P1.
- QUALIFY is P2 by default, because it's the core of how your ads turn into calls. It moves to P1 only when it's the failure mode's antidote (FM5 junk leads, FM10 long cycles); P2 is then OFFER or TARGET.
- SALES or SYSTEM is always last. SALES is the default, because it's half the product. Use SYSTEM only when the client didn't use the sales side.
- OFFER, SWITCH, and TARGET go in P1 or P2. P1 is always the failure mode's antidote (4B).
- The number of pillars equals the number of things the team actually did: two interventions, two pillars; three, three. This holds in all 11 of Daniel's scripts, whatever their length. With two: P1 is the antidote (with the ads and qualification folded in), then P2 is SALES.
- Asides never go in Pillar 3. The Meta aside (A8) is required in P1 or P2.
- Never describe the funnel in stages. You don't run separate top, middle, and bottom of funnel campaigns, so no script says you do.

**OFFER: offer and positioning**

- Label: "First, we rebuilt [his] offer." or "Second, we positioned [him] as the go-to [CATEGORY] company for [BUYER]."
- Body: "...so that {[he] looked like the obvious choice instead of just another vendor / buyers stopped comparing [him] on price / a [TITLE] who'd never heard of [him] still wanted to get on a call}."
- Add-ons: "We built it around [his] biggest results, like the work [he] did for [MARQUEE CLIENT]." / Aside A3.

**SWITCH: from cold outreach and referrals to Meta**

- Label: "First, we stopped the cold outreach that wasn't working." or "Next, we did a complete 180 on how [he] got clients."
- Body: asides A2 and A1, then "What we did instead was put [his] offer in front of [TITLES] with Meta ads."
- 180 version: "Before, [his] team was sending [N] cold emails a month that did take a ton of time, but they weren't even really getting replies. So we switched to Meta ads that put [his] offer in front of decision-makers who'd never heard of [him], and they started booking calls themselves."
- The Meta aside goes here whenever SWITCH is used.

**TARGET: one buyer**

- Labels: "First, we figured out exactly who [he] should be selling to." / "Market research and planning: we did a deep dive on [his] best clients."
- Body: "And every single one of them was a [COMPANY TYPE] with [SIZE], and the person who signs is the [TITLE]. So we went all in on that one buyer. So now every single ad speaks directly to the person who actually signs the deal."

**QUALIFY: pre-qualify and pre-educate before the call** (P2 by default)

- Label: "Second, we made sure every person was qualified before the call." or "Next, and this is the piece that most people miss, is making sure every single prospect is qualified and educated before they ever get on a call."
- Body: "So before, [he] was getting on calls with anyone who booked: wrong company size, no budget, people who didn't even know what [he] sells. But now, before anyone can book, we make sure they're qualified: [CRITERIA, e.g., the right company size, the budget, and the person who actually signs]. And then we educate them on [his] product, [his] offer, and [his] company, so they get on the call almost ready to buy."
- Outcome: "So now [his] team spends their time closing, not explaining."
- Junk-lead version (FM5, when QUALIFY is P1): "Before, [he] was getting leads, but [N]% of them were junk: wrong company size, fake numbers, people who never picked up. So now nobody gets on [his] calendar unless they're qualified, and by the time they do, they already know [his] product, [his] offer, and [his] company."
- Long-cycle version (FM10, when QUALIFY is P1): "Before, deals were taking [N] months because every call started from zero. So now every prospect is educated on [his] product, [his] offer, and [his] company before the call, and [his] deals close in [N] weeks instead."
- Pairs with A4 ("So leads don't equal revenue. I get it."). Add a proof number when you have one: share of calls that were qualified, show rate, or deals closed on the first or second call.

**DFY: run the whole engine** (always P1)

- Label: "First, we took the entire sales and marketing process off [his] plate." or "So first, we made it really easy for [him]."
- Body: "Before, [he] was doing everything [himself]: the outreach, the follow-ups, the proposals, the sales calls. It was way too much for any founder. So we immediately took almost all of it in-house: the offer, the ads, the qualification, the booking."

**SALES: help them close** (always last)

- Label: "And number three, we helped [his] team close." or "And finally, we worked with [his] sales team on the back end."
- Body: "Because getting calls is only half of it. So we gave [his] team a sales process, reviewed every call, and fixed where deals were stalling: [the pricing conversation, the follow-up, getting the partner on the call]. And [his] close rate went from [X]% to [Y]%."
- Never say the team was bad: "Before, [his] team was closing [X]%, which is good, but deals kept dying at [the pricing stage]."
- As the last pillar, it must end on what changed for the sales team: close rate before and after, deals closed, or days to close, with a number when you have one. "We mapped out every step" describes work, not what changed.

**SYSTEM: tracking and doubling down** (last, only when SALES isn't used)

- Label: "And number three, we systematized the entire thing."
- Body: "So we tracked every stage: cost per booked call, show rate, close rate. Before, there was no tracking; it was kind of throwing spaghetti at a wall, seeing what worked. But now we know exactly what a new client costs. We're doubling down on the winners week over week."

### 4D. Asides

Use 1–2 per script (max 3), counting A8. Never in Pillar 3. Every aside speaks to the viewer about their own situation, the way Daniel's do ("if you're posting tons of content without traction, please stop," "views don't equal dollars, I get it"). None of them talk about other agencies.

- **A1 Stop doing it:** "And so if that's you right now, please stop doing that right now." / "...please stop spinning your wheels."
- **A2 Sounds simple:** "And I know this sounds really simple, but like, I see founders do this every day." / "...but it is extremely difficult to execute properly."
- **A3 Non-negotiable:** "If you're selling a high-ticket B2B service, it is absolutely non-negotiable, because your offer is the first thing a decision-maker judges you on."
- **A4 Leads aren't revenue:** "So leads don't equal revenue. I get it."
- **A5 Most people miss it:** "...and this is the piece that most people miss..."
- **A6 Audience logic:** "Because the kind of people who reply to a cold email usually aren't the ones signing the deal. But the kind of people who see [his] offer and book a call themselves, those guys are already halfway to becoming a client."
- **A7 A pattern I see:** "And this is something I see with B2B founders a lot: they're really good at building, but they've never had to sell to strangers."
- **A8 Meta objection** (required): "[NAME]'s first reaction was, '[HIS OWN WORDS, e.g., My buyers aren't on Facebook].' But [his] buyers are people too, and they're scrolling Instagram at night just like everyone else. [N] of [his] [N] new clients came straight from those ads." Place it right after the first mention of Meta ads. Use the client's own words from intake. The proof number is mandatory.

### 4E. Final-state closers

- **F1 System** (default): "Now, [NAME] has a system that brings in qualified calls with decision-makers every single week."
- **F2 Dominance:** "And now [NAME] is absolutely dominating [his] niche. No one even comes close to [him] in the market that [he]'s in."
- **F3 Predictability:** "And this is the first time that [he]'s been actually able to predict [his] revenue instead of just waiting around, hoping a referral comes in."

## 5. Voice and number rules

**Openers, rhythm, pronouns, and word quotas (unchanged from Daniel):**

- About 3 in 10 sentences open with "And," 2 with "So," and 1 with "But" or "Now," landing at 55–68% overall.
- Median sentence is 18 words: about 20% under 10, about 28% over 25.
- "We" does every action. "I" appears at most twice. "You" appears only in asides, the guarantee line, closers, the scale proof, and the CTA.
- The featured name (founder or company) appears 3–5 times.
- Word quotas per ~400 words: "actually" ×2, "really" ×1–2, "consistently" ×1, "every single" ×1, "hundreds of" ×1–2, "system" ×1–3, plus 1–2 of "literally," "absolutely," "incredible," "super," or "tons of."

**Fixed vocabulary:** qualified calls, decision-makers, booked calls, sales pipeline, close rate, cost per booked call, the offer, Meta ads, qualified before the call, educated on the product, the offer, and the company, almost ready to buy, sales process, the back end, junk leads, referrals, the go-to [category] company, doubling down on the winners.

**Stock idioms (1–3 per script):** spinning your wheels, throwing spaghetti at a wall, put it on rails, to a T, A to Z, every step of the way, basically overnight, took off from there, blew past, absolutely crushed, eating into [his] profits, off [his] plate.

**Number rules:**

- Spell out "crore" and "lakh"; never write "Cr," "L," or "₹" in a spoken script. Use dollars only if the client bills in dollars.
- Crores take one decimal unless round (11.6 crore, 20 crore). Lakhs are whole (45 lakh).
- Before-numbers get minimizers (just, only, under, barely, never cracked). After-numbers get maximizers (over, plus, almost).
- The hedge-up ("lakhs, if not crores") is only for ongoing performance in L8 or a closer. It never replaces a result figure.
- Close rate and show rate are percentages. Growth and return on ad spend are multiples (3x, a 6x return). Ad spend is per month.

**Never:**

- prices or package names
- placeholders or vague stand-ins for missing numbers ("crores in new revenue")
- comments on other agencies
- two "But" sentences in a row pointing the same way
- the guarantee outside L13, or reworded
- competitor or agency names
- a question in the hook
- "Furthermore," "Additionally," or "In conclusion"
- more than three pillars
- "leads" in the hook
- funnel stages or "full funnel" (top, middle, or bottom of funnel)
- new-market or international expansion
- any knock on the client's product, delivery, or sales team

## 6. Build procedure

1. Fill the intake sheet and apply the fallbacks. If anything on the missing-data list is empty, stop and list it.
2. Pick the primary failure mode from 4B. Check 4G to confirm it's a fit pain.
3. Pick the hook variant from 4A and write L1 with clients, revenue, or pipeline numbers plus a time qualifier or kicker.
4. Write L2 (or skip it if the hook already says what the client does and there's nothing to add), then L3–L4 with one or two problems, then at most one L5 amplifier (pain math preferred). Check every "But" flips direction.
5. Add L6 if there's a breakout deal. Write L7, then L8 only if there's an ongoing rate not already stated.
6. Choose the L9 transition.
7. Choose the pillars: P1 is the antidote, P2 is QUALIFY by default, P3 is SALES by default.
8. Write the hero pillar (60–130 words), then the others (25–80 words), with at least one noun list. Place A8 in P1 or P2, using the client's own words and a proof number.
9. Write L13 (the guarantee line), then L14, L15, and L16.
10. Voice pass: openers, word quotas, 1–3 idioms, number formatting.
11. Run QA and trim to 350–450 words.

## 7. QA checklist

Every item must be yes.

**Length and hook**

- 350–450 words in 18–26 sentences?
- Hook is 21–41 words, opens "Here's how we" (or uses Before-Contrast), names the founder or company you chose, has 2+ exact numbers in clients, revenue, or pipeline, and no "leads"?
- No placeholders and no vague stand-ins anywhere?

**Story**

- Company type named by sentence 2, with the client's product, delivery, and team all protected?
- Setup names one or two problems, every one of them paid off by a pillar or the after-state, and every "But" flips direction?
- Identity line present only if the hook doesn't already say what the client does, or if it adds credibility or the flip?
- Before has a number? After has 2+ numbers and "consistently"? Escalator used only if there's a real overflow fact that isn't already the hook's headline?
- No hook number appears more than twice (pain-math milestone and Goal Crush goal are the only exceptions)?
- No two numbers contradict each other, and no ongoing rate was invented?

**Structure**

- Transition starts between 28% and 40% of the word count, and the scale proof between 83% and 92%?
- 2–3 pillars with ordinals, P1 answering the failure mode, QUALIFY in P2 (unless it's P1), SALES (or SYSTEM) last, and no funnel-stage language?
- Meta aside in P1 or P2, with the client's own words and a proof number? 1–3 asides total, none in Pillar 3?
- Guarantee appears exactly once, in L13, word for word, including "if you qualify"?
- Last pillar ends on what changed, and every pillar label matches its body?
- Final-state line and "click the link down below, see if you qualify"? (Scale proof optional.)
- Hero pillar includes a "why it works" line, and the pillar count matches the interventions?

**Voice and accuracy**

- 55–68% And/So/But/Now openers, word quotas met, featured name used 3–5 times, with matching pronouns throughout?
- Nothing from the "never" list, and every number and claim true for this client and for The Archer Media?

## 8. Worked example

A hypothetical client, written only from this spec.

**Intake.** Rohan runs a 40-person custom software company that builds for a Fortune 500 brand.

- **Before:** 100% of business from referrals, two new clients a year, and revenue barely moved from 7 to 8 crore in three years. His goal is 20 crore, and he's on every sales call.
- **After eight months:** 17 new clients, 11.6 crore in new revenue, 30+ qualified calls a month, and 14 of the 17 clients came from Meta.
- **Breakout:** a 45 lakh deal in week five.
- **Sales:** hired a closer, and the proposal close rate went from 18% to 29%.
- **Why it worked:** with Rohan on every call, nobody was building the pipeline.
- **Channel doubt:** his first reaction was "My buyers aren't on Facebook."
- **Qualification:** every call is pre-qualified on budget and on being the person who signs, and prospects are educated on the product, offer, and company before they book.

**Decisions:** From-To hook · FM2 (founder trapped), told as one problem: every client came through Rohan himself · pillars DFY with the A8 Meta aside → QUALIFY with A4 → SALES · closer F5 · CTA C1.

> [L1] Here's how we took our client, Rohan, from adding just two new clients a year to 17 new clients, 11.6 crore in new revenue, and over 30 qualified calls a month in just eight months. [L2] So Rohan runs a really successful 40-person software company that builds for a Fortune 500 brand. [L3] But before working with us, every client came through Rohan himself: his referrals, his network, his sales calls. [L4] He was adding just two new clients a year, and his revenue had barely moved from seven to eight crore in three years. [L5] And at that pace, it would take him over 35 years to hit his goal of 20 crore. [L6] But after just five weeks, he closed a 45 lakh deal, and his pipeline just took off from there. [L7] Now he has over 30 qualified calls booked every month, 11.6 crore in new revenue, and so many calls that he actually had to hire a closer. [L8] And he's consistently closing two new clients every single month.
>
> [L9] So here's the exact strategy that we used for Rohan. [P1 · DFY + A8 · hero] First, we took the entire sales and marketing process off his plate. Before, he was doing everything himself: the outreach, the follow-ups, the proposals, the sales calls. Because when the founder is on every call, nobody's actually building the pipeline. So we immediately took almost all of it in-house, starting with Meta ads. Rohan's first reaction was, "My buyers aren't on Facebook," but his buyers are people too, and they're scrolling Instagram at night just like everyone else. Fourteen of his 17 new clients came straight from those ads. [P2 · QUALIFY + A4] Second, we made sure every person was qualified before the call, because leads don't equal revenue. I get it. So before anyone can book, we make sure they've got the budget and they're the person who actually signs. And then we educate them on his product, his offer, and his company, so they get on the call almost ready to buy. [P3 · SALES] And number three, we helped his team close. So we gave his new closer a sales process, fixed where deals were stalling, and his proposal close rate went from 18% to 29%.
>
> [L13] And the best part is, you don't have to take a blind bet on us. If you qualify, we guarantee a 10 to 30 crore increase in revenue in 12 months, or your money back. [L14 · F5] Now he's fully out of sales, and the whole system runs without him. [L15] And we've generated hundreds of crores in sales pipeline for B2B companies just like his, and we do this every single day. [L16 · C1] So if you're still doing all the sales calls yourself, just click the link down below, see if you qualify.

**Measured against the spec:** 448 words in 26 sentences, 62% And/So/But/Now openers, the transition at 37% and the scale proof at 91%. The pain math checks out: a gain of 1 crore over three years puts the remaining 12 crore at 36 years.
